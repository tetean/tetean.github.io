A Brief Rest after Being Busy

photography research shenzhen

Back in August, I wrapped up my very first CCF-A paper. Honestly, it was not easy at all -- much tougher than the earlier things I have worked on. And to be fair, I am not even sure if it will get accepted. In computer science, the first author usually takes on about 80% of the workload, which feels very different from what I have heard happens in other fields. For me, this whole process was a mix of struggle and learning, and I know I still have a long way to go.

I still keep this little dream in mind -- that maybe one day I will go for a PhD. For now, I just see this paper as a small step forward. Finishing a project does not mean everything is done; it is more like letting it go and getting ready for what is next. I tell myself not to get stuck, but to keep moving, even if it is just one step at a time.

Of course, life is not only about work. Sometimes you just need to pause, take a breath, and recharge a little. That is why I took a short trip to Shenzhen -- it is not far from where I study. And guess what? It was actually my first time seeing the sea. Standing there with the breeze in my face, listening to the waves... it felt so refreshing and meaningful in its own quiet way. Moments like that remind me that growth does not only come from research -- it also comes from living, experiencing, and enjoying life a bit along the way.

My First IELTS Experience

language milestone

Upon my admission to graduate school, I made a conscious decision to enhance my English proficiency. Commencing my endeavor in late September, I diligently prepared for the IELTS examination, culminating in my test-taking experience in December.

Reflecting on the journey of language acquisition, I realized the significance of my prior academic exposure since the third grade. However, it was during the intensive two-month preparation for the IELTS exam that I truly grasped the depth of knowledge and skills necessary for proficiency.

Engaging with English has become an integral part of my daily routine, transcending the confines of exam preparation to enrich my life with diverse perspectives and experiences. It is through this continual engagement that I envision attaining not only linguistic mastery but also a deeper connection with the global community.

Winter in the North

life reflection
The snow came late this year. There is a special feeling for winter in the north. Looking at the pedestrians on the road, everyone walks forward, clothes thick, each step is not too hurried, and each step must be stepped on. The fast-paced life seems to be slowing down. It is said that with age, people are more and more unconscious of the passage of time, and whenever it dawns on them, they realize that time is not on their side. The third winter in the Northeast is still a novelty.
